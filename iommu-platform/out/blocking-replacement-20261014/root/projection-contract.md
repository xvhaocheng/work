## Two admissible analyses and one unresolved mechanism

The fixed-DAG, one-affected-node specialization is a fully specified analytical BR1 program: completed-history weights, fixed resource edges, all named release sinks, a single additional walk-delay d at a node, and no subsequent residency/order change. Its exact cheaper representation is B7-StaticCP; the root accepts that explicit ordinary coverage, not published complete-BR1 priority.

For a truly complete small copy-release horizon, protect all shared-lookup keys H still needed before the named releases. If a legal outside-H victim exists, and subsequent fills preserve H or there are no further conflicting fills, that replacement creates no new lookup/memory change. B8-Hpin supplies this same zero-impact baseline with simpler state. The native two-source64B example has few H keys, but this alone does not establish global optimality: dynamic arrival reshaping can make an induced miss change other owners' progress, and later unprotected fills can change PLRU behavior. Only the control file's explicit additional monotonic/tie conditions yield exact best-action coverage.

The victim-dependent dynamic arrival/release program remains a positive mechanism proposal. This round does not have a reachable signed complete-gain chain, a history-to-shared-service decomposition, or a charged production projector demonstrating improvement beyond B7/B8/localcost/owner/next-use. Accordingly its performance entry is not admitted; no model or guessed timing is used to fill these gaps. This is a bounded design decision, not a theorem that all dynamic interaction policies are ordinary-covered or useless.

## Owner-granularity result

At current native request/copy/B receipt releases, next-use information can define a short horizon, but there are ordinarily outside-H victims. At a multi-copy software task's final buffer release, the horizon may include many later translations; immediate next-use alone does not define them. A future contract would have to document an actual currently-owned cross-copy resource, every necessary intervening use and legal feedback/cost, without importing a whole future trace as free input. Merely changing the name of copy B to task release, keeping registration alive, or adding more artificial concurrent work is not such evidence.

No automatic successor expands this horizon, changes64B certificate eligibility, captures the oldAW prefix, tunes RCS1 work, creates a new source-only goal, or starts RTL/FPGA. Preserve the positive BR1 rule, exact reduced fixed-graph form, conditional Hpin proof, new functional cold dependency graph and native certificate correction for the next user-directed design decision.
