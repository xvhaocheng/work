To identify the tasks in \nthe critical path, most of these works use the concept of \nupward rank and downward rank. The upward rank of a task \nis the maximum sum of computation and communication cost \nof the tasks in the dependency chains from that task to an exit \nnode in the graph. The downward rank of a task is the \nmaximum sum of computation and communication cost of the \ntasks in the dependency chain from an entry node in the graph \nup to that task. Each task has an upward rank and downward \nrank for each processor type in the heterogeneous system, as \nthe computation and communication costs differ across \nprocessor types. \n \nThe Heterogeneous Earliest Finish Time (HEFT) algorithm \n[4] maintains a list of tasks sorted in decreasing order of their \nupward rank. At each schedule step, HEFT assigns the task \nwith the highest upward rank to the processor that finishes the \nexecution of the task at the earliest possible time. Another \nwork is the Longest Dynamic Critical Path (LDCP) algorithm \n[1]. LDCP also statically schedules first the task with the \nhighest upward rank on every schedule step.
