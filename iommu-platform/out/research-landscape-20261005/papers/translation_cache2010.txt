Generally speaking, earlier studies concluded that organizations
based on hashing, such as the inverted page table, outperformed
organizations based upon radix trees for supporting large virtual
address spaces [23, 15]. However, these studies did not take into
account the possibility of caching page table entries from the higher
levels of the radix tree. This paper shows that radix tables cause up
to 20% fewer total memory accesses and up to 400% fewer DRAM
accesses than hash-based tables because of the locality in virtual
address use.
