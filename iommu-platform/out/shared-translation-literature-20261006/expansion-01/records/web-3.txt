### Abstract

In order to meet Sustainable Development Goal (SDG 7), Bangladesh needs to find a solution to provide reliable electrification in the remote hilly region of Bandarban. This paper attempts to find a profound off-grid renewable energy solution to the ongoing electricity crisis of Mirinja hill-range of Bandarban district by utilizing huge solar, wind and biofuel potential of Mirinja range. 
Keywords: Renewable energy, Off-grid electricity, HOMER, Wind turbine, Solar energy.

1. INTRODUCTION

Recent advancements in die-stacking technology have en-

abled the integration of a sizable amount of low-power logic

into conventional DRAM chips [1, 2], reviving the decades

old idea of processing in memory [3, 4, 5, 6]. The logic

in memory devices can leverage the physical data proximity

and high internal bandwidth to perform memory-intensive


tributed Inverted Page Table (DIPTA) translation mecha-

nism. In DIPTA, each memory partition keeps the transla-

tion information for its share next to the data. DIPTA lim-

its the associativity of VM so that all but a few bits remain

invariant across the virtual-to-physical mapping, and uses

highly-accurate way prediction (i.e., predicting the unknown

bits upon an access) to enable address translation to com-

plete together with data fetch. DIPTA avoids the need for

conventional translation hardware—such as TLBs, MMU

caches, and page walkers—on the MPUs, and completely

eliminates the ever-growing performance overhead of trans-

lation. DIPTA requires no changes to the CPU chips, and is

fully compatible with conventional page tables, which CPUs

still use to access any location in the MPU’s memory the

same way they access conventional memory.
