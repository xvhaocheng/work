\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Measured shared-translation performance.} Bermuda demonstrates cross-DMA interference on Arm SMMUv2 and evaluates transfer-size dependence with translation enabled and disabled~\cite{bermuda2025}.\claim{L-BERMUDA} Koenig et al. study RISC-V shared virtual addressing on FPGA, including host noise, PTW time, and caching of the page-table path~\cite{koenig2025}.\claim{L-DATE} Rodr\'{i}guez's thesis supplies the closest platform predecessor: multiple iDMA engines, IOMMU performance evaluation, cache-capacity effects, and recognition of device contention~\cite{rodriguez2024}.\claim{L-RODRIGUEZ} Our comparison concerns sustained autonomous traffic and the internally observed service dependency, not a claim that these works lacked performance evaluation.

\textbf{Direct concurrent-client precedents.} Zhao et al. implement distributed I/O address-translation caches (IOATCs) sharing an I/O address-translation server (IOATS), evaluate multi-IOATC scaling, and provide internal hit/miss waveforms and FPGA network validation~\cite{zhao2025}.\claim{L-PROC} Hur and Ro run concurrent client mixtures, evaluate shared-IOTLB replacement policies, and study an adaptive policy using a RISC-V processor configuration with an SMMU model~\cite{hur2025}.\claim{L-HUR} These directly overlap the shared-translation performance question. Relative to their reported evaluations, our focus is a fixed-neighbor control and a request-identified response intervention connecting another stream's PTW service to a hit's waiting time.\claim{L-DELTA} Their simulation or architectural differences do not remove them from the comparison.

\textbf{Mechanism and observation history.} HERO already provides internal translation-event visibility and hit-under-miss behavior; Kurth et al. discuss miss-related buffering that can block subsequent hits, as well as prefetching and DMA support~\cite{hero2017,kurth2018}.\claim{L-PREHISTORY} We therefore claim implementation-specific evidence and quantified limits. Neither hit-under-miss, shared-service blocking, partitioning, rate control, nor the non-additivity of optimizations is presented as a newly discovered general principle.\claim{L-DELTA}
