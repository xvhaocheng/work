\section{Platform and Methodology}
\subsection{Fixed repaired baseline}
The platform instantiates a CVA6 host, four DMA engines, a shared RISC-V IOMMU with a 16-entry original replacement structure, and a double-data-rate (DDR) memory path on a Genesys2 FPGA. The evaluated clock is 50\,MHz.\claim{M-PLATFORM} The primary measurements activate a victim and one aggressor; the presence of four instantiated engines is not a four-active-device result.\claim{G-COVERAGE}

The baseline uses the original IOTLB organization with necessary platform and correctness fixes, explicit autonomous batch supply, and passive path observation. It excludes the partitioning, bypass, composition, banked-service, and request-intervention extensions used in other historical configurations.\claim{M-IDENTITY} We do not obtain an ``original'' baseline merely by disabling switches in a research tree. Each configuration has a fixed source manifest, build configuration, software payload, and result package; historical runs retain their separate identities and any missing identity fields.\claim{M-IDENTITY}

Both new configurations share one software payload. DS0 directly connects the data-structure path. DS50 separately enables a 50/50-cycle diagnostic delayer on that path.\claim{M-DS} The path serves more than page-table requests, and the resulting service schedule can change miss behavior. Thus DS0 is the original-wiring hardware result, while DS50 tests sensitivity to added data-structure-path latency. Neither the DS0--DS50 difference nor A1--A8 subtraction is a pure PTW-cost estimate.\claim{M-DS}

\subsection{Workload and controls}
The baseline keeps the victim task fixed: 4096 copies of 4\,KiB after a 256-item warm-up. The useful copied payload is 16\,MiB.\claim{M-WORKLOAD} Autonomous hardware supply keeps the batch running without requiring the CPU to issue and poll each individual descriptor. This is an explicit workload fixture and changes the offered traffic relative to a software-fed loop.\claim{M-SUPPLY} Copy throughput counts useful copied payload, not the sum of read and write memory traffic.\claim{M-STAT}

Solo, A1, A8, A16, and A64 preserve the victim's configuration while changing whether and how the neighbor runs.\claim{M-CONTROLS} A1 is a conventional experimental control with zero measured misses on both sides in the new baseline.\claim{R-MISS} It demonstrates that a substantial co-run loss exists without measured misses; it does not make the remaining A1--A8 contrast a translation-only intervention. The latter also changes the address stream and its interaction with data service.\claim{M-CONTROLS}

The completed baseline contains \BaselineTotalRuns{} accepted trials, with \RunsPerCondition{} trials per DS/condition.\claim{M-BASELINE} Collection followed DS50--DS0--DS50, with 25/50/25 trials over three actual boots. DS0's condition repeats share one boot; each DS50 condition pools five trials from each of the two DS50 boots, and the segments remain separately traceable.\claim{M-BASELINE} The repeats describe variation within these collected runs, rather than independent reboot sampling.

\subsection{Timing and passive observers}
Hardware elapsed time uses the victim's hardware observation window. Software elapsed time includes distinct control and polling effects and is reported as a separate metric. HPM miss counters are read through non-atomic software snapshots, which need not have exactly the hardware window's boundaries.\claim{M-OBSERVER} A zero HPM count alone does not prove the first lookup of every hardware-window request hit. Conversely, small nonzero software-window counts cannot locate those misses inside the timed batch.

The path observer follows read and write address events through four stages: ingress presentation to acceptance, internal passage to translated-address presentation, translated-address backpressure to acceptance, and subsequent completion. The read completion boundary and write completion boundary follow their respective response protocols.\claim{M-PATH} Internal passage includes waiting, translation service, and departure-related effects; it is not a PTW timer. The final write stage also includes write-data supply and response effects.\claim{M-PATH}

For a stage in trial $i$, let $S_i$ be the accumulated lifetime of complete observed transactions and $N_i$ their count. The plotted mean is $S_i/N_i$, followed by the median across trials.\claim{M-STAT} Complete and cross-window transactions are distinguished by the observer rather than silently clipping a lifetime.\claim{M-PATH} Read and write activity and multiple in-flight transactions overlap. Stage means therefore cannot be stacked into fractions of total elapsed slowdown, and accumulated sums or maxima cannot reconstruct a latency cumulative distribution function (CDF).\claim{M-STAT}

\subsection{Controlled historical evidence and statistics}
Historical E3 experiments use a separate 64-byte diagnostic task and identify the aggressor request, selected PTW response, and waiting victim request. A one-response delay intervention is enabled or disabled within complementary pairs; the victim's first-lookup hit and its own translation-service interval are observed separately.\claim{R-E3} These pairs test a local dependency. They are not equivalent to the new sustained-batch workload and do not apportion its complete slowdown.

We report descriptive medians and the recorded repeat count. Throughput is calculated for each trial before aggregation. Reported extra elapsed slowdown is
\begin{equation}
  100\left(\frac{\operatorname{median}(T_{A_k})}{\operatorname{median}(T_{A1})}-1\right),
\end{equation}
using matching DS and source configuration.\claim{M-STAT} It is a ratio of medians, not a median of paired ratios. Historical paired interventions retain their own pairing and sample counts. Where error bars are shown, they are percentile-bootstrap intervals for the reported median or contrast, resampled within the observed boots with a fixed seed.\claim{M-CI} They describe these collected runs, not a population of independent reboots, an equivalence test, or a worst-case guarantee.\claim{M-STAT}
